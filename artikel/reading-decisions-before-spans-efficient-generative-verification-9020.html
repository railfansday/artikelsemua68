Reading Decisions Before Spans: Efficient Generative Verification

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 25 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (176 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.32931v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: A developer checking an assistant's report may need only an alarm that a claim is unsupported. A generative verifier, however, can continue decoding to quote and classify the errors. We study how much of that output is needed to recover its decision. In a frozen 2B span verifier, the choice between an empty error list and its first object precedes all localization. Native8 stops generation at this branch. The preceding seven syntax tokens are fixed, so Prefill1 supplies them as input and generates only the unrestricted branch token, using the same native instruction. On 2,015 code-agent TEST responses, Prefill1 reduces eager resident service by 84.84% relative to full generation, with released-reference F1 of 83.43 versus 83.41. It saves 31.98% over Native8 even though their logical token totals are identical. Under default compiled execution, the extra saving is 6.74-26.73% on the complete workload, with small decision changes. Controlled evidence interventions and first-location decoding establish what these shorter readouts retain and what requires further generation.
