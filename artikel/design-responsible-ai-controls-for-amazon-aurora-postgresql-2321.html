However, it also introduces challenges around privacy, accuracy, safety, and governance, especially when the underlying data contains sensitive customer information. In this post, we map the eight AWS Responsible AI (RAI) dimensions to Aurora PostgreSQL capabilities. We then walk through a design pattern for the Explainability dimension, using a Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) based customer support chatbot as the reference use case. This post implements one dimension, Explainability, in depth. Treat it as Part 1 of a series rather than a full build-out of all eight.

In a recent development, amazon Aurora PostgreSQL-Compatible Edition makes it more straightforward to operationalize responsible AI through its native Aurora Machine Learning (Aurora ML) capabilities. With Aurora ML, you can invoke Amazon SageMaker endpoints and Amazon Bedrock foundation models directly from SQL, without data movement, custom extract, transform, and load (ETL) pipelines, or complex integrations. This post uses Aurora ML to generate vector embeddings from SQL, keeping the embedding pipeline inside the database layer.

Industry observers note that AWS defines eight core Responsible AI dimensions, published through the AWS Well-Architected Responsible AI Lens, Amazon Bedrock Guardrails, and the Responsible Use of AI Guide. These dimensions (Fairness, Explainability, Privacy and Security, Safety, Controllability, Veracity and Robustness, Governance, and Transparency) provide a structured framework for evaluating and implementing trustworthy AI systems.

It has been revealed that customer support systems often struggle to provide accurate, context-aware responses while maintaining privacy and compliance. Consider a customer support chatbot built on Amazon Aurora PostgreSQL that uses RAG with pgvector embeddings, Amazon Bedrock model inference, and AWS Responsible AI guardrails to deliver grounded, explainable responses.

A customer can ask questions such as “Why was my last bill high?” or “Where is my last order?” and receive accurate answers with citations, while sensitive data remains obscured through personally identifiable information (PII) redaction and access controls.

Industry observers note that an ingestion pipeline redacts PII from support tickets, FAQs, and product documents, then converts them into vector embeddings. When a user submits a query, AWS Lambda runs a semantic search over Aurora PostgreSQL, applies Amazon Bedrock Guardrails for privacy and grounding, sends the guarded context to an Amazon Bedrock foundation model (FM), and logs every interaction for explainability and governance.

Lambda grounds responses in enterprise data, redacts PII and applies Row-Level Security (RLS) for privacy, records citations and interaction logs for explainability, and writes audit records for governance.

Industry observers note that when to build this versus use a managed service: Amazon Bedrock Knowledge Bases provides built-in RAG with citations and is the simpler choice when default retrieval and citation behavior meets your needs. Build the custom logging design in this post when you need provenance stored in your own schema. That provenance includes retrieved versus cited sources, similarity scores, and guardrail traces that you can query with SQL and retain under your own policies.
