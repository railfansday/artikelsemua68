Today we are announcing the general availability of the trusted access program API on Amazon Bedrock. The API gives you one place to discover, request, and enroll in model provider trusted access programs. We are also announcing the public preview of the first two offerings for Claude models: Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (EFS) and Enterprise Data Controls (EDC). Both are available on Amazon Bedrock, including Claude Fable, Claude Mythos, and Claude Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 used through the Cyber Verification Program (CVP).

Industry observers note that frontier AI labs require stricter safety monitoring policies to their most capable models, and those policies still apply on Amazon Bedrock. For eligible customers, model providers also offer customers more control on how those policies are met, such as where retained data is stored and how it is reviewed. Each offering has its own terms, approvals, and settings.

Sources indicate that the Amazon Bedrock trusted access program API gives every provider offering one consistent Amazon Bedrock experience. Your teams discover offerings, check eligibility, and enroll in the same way for every provider. Your administrators decide who can enroll, using existing IAM policies and service control policies. As more model providers add offerings, those appear in the same place.

Industry observers note that EFS and EDC were built in partnership between AWS and Anthropic. They let you access Anthropic’s most capable models safely, from both a model use and a customer data perspective. With both EFS and EDC, when automated safety monitoring finds a risk, it creates a Safety Case for your team to review. With EFS, customers store their data in their own AWS account, and prompts and completions are not retained by Anthropic or Amazon Bedrock. With EDC, retained data is stored in Amazon-managed storage instead of your account and prompts and completions are not retained by Anthropic. One application gives access to both offerings.

In this post, we explain how the trusted access program API works, then walk through EFS and EDC storage options and setup.

According to recent reports, A trusted access program is a named provider program that gives customers more control of safety reviews and access to model capabilities in exchange for accepting the program’s terms. Amazon Bedrock trusted access program API now gives you one place to find the model and use case programs available to your account. EFS and EDC are the first offerings available today. Offerings that providers add later appear in the same place and follow the same steps.

It has been revealed that anyone with access to the trusted access program API can list the offerings and check their status for an account, an organization in AWS Organizations, or an Amazon Bedrock project. The status tells you what to do next. eligible means you’re eligible for the offering and can enroll, requestable means you need approval for eligibility first, and the statusReason field in the response explains how to request it. The model provider (or AWS, as authorized by the model provider) approves your eligibility.

Enrolling is a separate step where you accept the offering’s terms imposed by the provider. You enroll at the scope where you were approved: an account, every account in an organization from the management account, or a single project. You can opt out at any time. Each step is a call to the trusted access program API, so you control who can enroll with AWS Identity and Access Management (IAM) policies and service control policies (SCPs). More information is available in the trusted access programs documentation.

With Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (EFS), you can retain your data in your own AWS environment with full control. It is designed with over 100 customers, including a quarter of the Fortune 100 and many companies in industries with strict rules on data visibility, like healthcare, financial services, and law.

In a recent development, automated safety monitoring looks for patterns that only show up across many requests. These include intentional misuse that could lead to serious harm, such as offensive cyber operations. They also include agents that go beyond what a user asked for, such as using a software exploit to reach an internal system.

Sources indicate that on Amazon Bedrock, AWS operates EFS and the customer inputs and outputs stay within the customer’s AWS account. Only automated safety monitoring within AWS reads the retained inputs and outputs. AWS human reviewers don’t review content for EFS. Your review stays with you. Neither AWS nor Anthropic humans access your retained inputs or outputs. Your inputs and outputs are never used for training.

Amazon Bedrock reaches your AWS Key Management Service (AWS KMS) key and Amazon Simple Storage Service (Amazon S3) bucket as a single AWS service principal and only through the key policy and bucket policy that you write. You can remove that access at any time, but Amazon Bedrock stops serving the covered models 24 hours after it loses access to your key or bucket.

EDC offers the same automated safety monitoring as EFS and human review of flagged activity stays with your team. Unlike EFS, EDC stores retained data in Amazon managed storage, encrypted with AWS managed keys, so there’s no bucket or key to set up. Eligible customers can access both EFS and EDC through a single enrollment application. If your policy allows retained data to stay in Amazon-managed storage and encrypted with AWS-managed keys, enroll in EDC. If your policy instead requires retained data to be encrypted with your AWS KMS key and stay in your S3 bucket, enroll in EFS. Either way, your team will be able to review flagged activity.
