Novo Nordisk (Novo) is one of the world’s leading global pharmaceutical companies, manufacturing medicines for chronic diseases such as diabetes and obesity, and delivering them to patients across many countries. Behind every product lies a manufacturing network that adheres to strict quality requirements. Every quality-relevant process step must be described, controlled, and validated through formal documentation. Keeping this documentation current and accurate is a core responsibility of the Manufacturing Technology team within the Tablet area, a responsibility that spans a large and diverse portfolio of controlled documents, including standard operating procedures, work instructions, forms, and training materials, across the global organization.

Sources indicate that managing this portfolio presents significant challenges. Document updates are frequent, driven by regulatory changes, process improvements, equipment modifications, and audit findings. Each update demands careful review, structured approval workflows, and timely communication to all affected stakeholders. With thousands of active documents spread across multiple divisions, departments, and product programs, tracking which documents are due for revision, pending approval, or require immediate attention becomes increasingly complex. Much of this oversight right now relies on manual processes and fragmented tracking methods such as spreadsheets or disconnected systems. These methods are time-consuming, error-prone, and difficult to scale. The result is a risk of outdated documentation remaining in circulation, delayed updates, and critical gaps in compliance visibility.

It has been revealed that beyond document management, there is a deeper knowledge challenge. Valuable manufacturing knowledge is distributed across many controlled documents, versions, sites, and product programs. Finding the right answer at the right time often depends on experienced individuals knowing where to look. This dependency on tribal knowledge creates bottlenecks that slow down decision-making and, ultimately, impact how promptly life-saving medicines reach patients.

To address these challenges, the Manufacturing Technology team within the Tablet area has embarked on developing an intelligent support system powered by agentic AI, deployed on AWS. The goal is to reduce lead times, improve documentation visibility, and make critical manufacturing knowledge more accessible, ultimately helping the organization move faster and serve more patients around the world. This post dives deep into the architecture and the key design decisions made to meet the high standards of accuracy and traceability required in a regulated pharmaceutical environment. The solution helped subject-matter experts (SMEs) complete work that used to take hours or days in minutes.

We also describe an AI process-intelligence assistant that can answer complex process questions with grounded, cited responses. The solution is built around a GraphRAG architecture, combining a knowledge graph with hybrid search to retrieve the most relevant and contextually accurate information. AI agents, built on a managed agent framework, coordinate the retrieval and response process end-to-end. A guided mode helps clarify ambiguous questions before a search is initiated, helping make responses more precise and defensible.

It has been revealed that teams supporting manufacturing repeatedly answer variations of the same question: what does the process require, for this site, this product, and this equipment? Answering well requires four things at once:.

Recent findings suggest that conventional keyword search returns documents, not answers, with no concept of scope, version, or relationship. A plain vector-based Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) system improves recall on natural-language questions but still struggles with exact identifiers (document numbers, equipment codes), with multi-hop relationship questions, and (most importantly for a regulated domain) with identifying when relevant content is not available. The team needed a system that treats structure and relationships as first-class, stays grounded in approved sources, and clarifies instead of guessing.

In a recent development, the assistant is built on a GraphRAG architecture: a knowledge graph in Amazon Neptune captures structure and relationships, and a hybrid search index in Amazon OpenSearch Serverless provides semantic and exact-match recall over document contents. The two stores are deliberately kept separate and bridged at query time rather than combined into a single engine. This is a common production GraphRAG pattern.

Recent findings suggest that the reasoning layer runs on Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, a platform to build, connect, and optimize agents at scale with any framework or model. AgentCore was chosen for its managed agent runtime with built-in session isolation, native tool orchestration, and integrated AWS Identity and Access Management (IAM) and observability. This removes undifferentiated infrastructure work so the team could focus on the domain logic. The agent runtimes are built with the Strands Agents SDK and powered by Anthropic Claude models on Amazon Bedrock. Documents are processed through an ingestion pipeline that uses Amazon Bedrock Data Automation for multimodal parsing, orchestrated with AWS Step Functions and AWS Lambda. The stack is defined as infrastructure as code with the AWS Cloud Development Kit (AWS CDK).

The graph is organized around two complementary layers, which is what lets the assistant take into account where a document sits and what it is about.

In a recent development, high-level taxonomy: The process tree. The organization’s official process framework is modeled as a hierarchy of process nodes (roughly three levels, parent to child). Each controlled document is attached to the lowest applicable process level, so each onboarded document has a known place. This lets the assistant scope a question to a process area before retrieving anything, and automatically categorize newly ingested documents.

Industry observers note that low-level taxonomy: Domain entities. SMEs define the entities that make an answer correct or incorrect in manufacturing: sites, product generations, equipment, process steps, and similar attributes. These are modeled as first-class graph nodes linked to the documents that describe them, using a small set of unified relationship types rather than an ever-growing list of edge names.

Industry observers note that modeling both layers is what distinguishes a genuine GraphRAG system from two databases side by side. The document number on an OpenSearch passage is also a node in the graph, so a search hit is an immediate entry point into the graph’s structure with no fuzzy re-matching required. This chunk-to-graph bridge is a key design choice in the retrieval path.

According to recent reports, early GraphRAG work favored open-ended extraction, letting a language model discover entities with no fixed schema. This approach is powerful for open-ended sensemaking over sprawling text, but it is noisy, hard to maintain, and difficult to audit. Over the past couple of years, the consensus has shifted toward ontology-guided extraction using a predefined, domain-specific schema to guide the model, which improves accuracy and reliability for specialized corpora.

It has been revealed that for regulated pharmaceutical manufacturing this is not a compromise, but the right default. The domain already has a real ontology, curated by experts, and a fixed schema with a human-in-the-loop promotion process for newly introduced entities supports the auditability the team needed in a regulated pharmaceutical environment. The design aligns with where much of the field has moved since early open-schema approaches, while making design decisions easier to explain to quality and audit stakeholders.

A reranking stage (a cross-encoder through Amazon Bedrock Rerank) sits after fusion: it reads the question and each candidate passage together and reorders them, adding a precision pass on top of the cheaper recall stage before anything reaches the model.

According to recent reports, A distinctive part of the assistant is how it handles ambiguity. A question like “which samples are taken on the granulator?” has several correct answers depending on product generation, site, equipment, and batch size. Answering immediately risks a confident, wrong, out-of-scope response, which is the worst outcome in a regulated setting.

It has been revealed that guided mode inverts the usual RAG order: instead of retrieving first, the agent narrows scope through clarification first, then searches. This follows recent research on clarifying ambiguous questions before retrieval and on using a knowledge graph to supply the clarification options rather than letting the model invent them. In a recent development, the wider vault this solution sits on top of holds on the order of four million documents. At that scale, the problem is not a shortage of information but the near-impossibility of finding the exactly right, in-scope, current answer by hand. Traditional enterprise search returns long lists ranked by keyword overlap and leaves the hard work to the person asking. This includes deciding which result applies, which version is effective, and how documents relate.

The design is intended to scale with the vault rather than degrade as it grows: the larger and more interconnected the documentation, the more value the graph and scoping add.

Depending on the type of task an SME is doing (searching for an answer, updating a document, or adapting one for a fresh context), work that used to take hours or days is reduced to minutes. Beyond raw time saved, the solution changes what is practical to do at all:.

Just as notable in a regulated setting, the assistant’s design to clarify scope and flag missing or ambiguous content helps reduce the risk of confident, out-of-scope answers, where the real cost of a mistake lies.

The Novo Manufacturing Technology team within the Tablet area collaborated with AWS to build this solution, with subject-matter experts and data and AI engineers working closely throughout. The taxonomy that makes the assistant accurate comes directly from the group’s domain experts. The retrieval and agent architecture was designed and iterated with AWS.

It has been revealed that building a prototype was key to getting the solution to go live. Rather than debating capabilities in the abstract, the team put a working system in front of subject-matter experts early. They tested it against real questions and used what they learned to sharpen the taxonomy, the retrieval path, and the guided-mode behavior. The prototype turned an ambitious idea into something people could try, critique, and trust. It also de-risked the architecture decisions before any broader rollout.

Recent findings suggest that by combining a curated knowledge graph, hybrid search, and a clarify-before-retrieve agent on Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, the Novo Manufacturing Technology team within the Tablet area built a process-intelligence assistant that is fast where speed helps and grounded where accuracy matters. The design applies current GraphRAG patterns to regulated manufacturing: ontology-guided extraction, a bridged two-store retrieval path, graph-grounded disambiguation, and reranking. This turns a large documentation landscape into grounded, cited answers with room to extend to new domains.
