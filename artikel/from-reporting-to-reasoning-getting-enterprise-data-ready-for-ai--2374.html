AWS Quick User Group DC inaugural meetup recap.

Sources indicate that many organizations are asking how to move beyond static reporting dashboards and begin using generative and agentic AI. But before an AI agent can answer questions or help teams act, it requires a trusted data foundation.

Figure 1: AWS Quick User Group DC inaugural meetup, October 2026.

On Wednesday, October 7, 2026, data leaders, developers, and practitioners gathered at the AWS Skills Center in Arlington, Virginia, for the AWS Quick User Group DC meetup. The session brought together founders, software engineers, and enterprise leaders, among them a team from Financial Independence Group (FIG). Attendees explored practical strategies for modernizing legacy architectures, preparing structured data for agentic workflows, and automating routine tasks with no-code tools.

User groups like this provide a forum for peer-to-peer learning and collaborative problem-solving. The event offered attendees an inside look at how organizations are putting AWS Quick to work to drive productivity gains.

For many in the room, this meetup was a first look at what agentic AI looks like in practice with AWS Quick.

Figure 2: Arun Ganesan presenting what’s new with AWS Quick.

Industry observers note that the meetup kicked off by exploring the latest product capabilities across AWS Quick, focusing on its evolution into a unified AI assistant for work. Rather than functioning solely as a traditional business intelligence visualization tool, AWS Quick unifies analytics, natural-language interaction, and multi-step task orchestration.

The core of the day highlighted a real-world customer journey showing how one enterprise is rethinking its data architecture so AI agents can work from governed, trusted data.

Vanessa Gonzalez, Vice President of AI at Financial Independence Group (FIG), shared how FIG keeps pushing past what’s possible today. They’re adopting upcoming tools and platforms as technology changes, and building toward AI-powered analytics and agentic systems on top of their data with AWS Quick.

Figure 3: Vanessa Gonzalez, Vice President of AI at FIG, presenting.

Technology is changing fast, and FIG wants to be at the front of that change. Building on its existing analytics, FIG moved to AWS Quick, a platform that brings AI into its dashboards, reporting, and analytics. AWS Quick is FIG’s “single front door” for enterprise analytics. It brings together structured data such as customer relationship management (CRM) systems, databases, and finance data. It also pulls in unstructured content such as internal knowledge bases, policy documents, and collateral, through a governed data layer. That gives FIG a base for AI-powered insights and agentic workflows built on its own data.

One of the key takeaways is how swiftly teams can go from an idea to a working solution with AWS Quick. Newly introduced ideas now take hours or even minutes to build, not weeks or months.

The event concluded with an open group discussion where attendees shared practical observations on bridging the gap between legacy infrastructure and generative AI.

As organizations move toward agentic architectures, the consensus from the DC user group was clear: successful AI adoption isn’t only about selecting a foundation model. It starts with intentional data design, unified access, and empowering teams to build practical automations. Building fast isn’t only about tools. It also means learning from each other, which is why events like this matter.
