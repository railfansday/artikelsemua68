Recent findings suggest that voice cloning in Amazon Polly is now generally available as a gated release. Give Amazon Polly a few minutes of audio recordings from a speaker who has agreed to have their voice cloned, and Amazon Polly builds your own cloned voice in that speaker’s voice. The cloned voices run on the Amazon Polly generative engine, which delivers similar naturalness and accuracy as the rest of the generative voices. Amazon Polly voice cloning launches with seven languages: English, Spanish, German, French, Italian, Portuguese, and Hindi. Each cloned voice speaks the language it was recorded in. The cloned voices are automatically encrypted and access-controlled: only you can access your cloned voices.

In a recent development, amazon Polly voice cloning is available in all the AWS Regions supported by the Amazon Polly generative engine, including the AWS GovCloud (US) Region. Access is already enabled for select Amazon Polly customers. Others can reach out to their AWS account team or apply for access to get access.

Industry observers note that with this upcoming feature addition, you can build the cloning lifecycle into AWS applications. Building a cloned voice is free for Amazon Polly customers, and Amazon Polly charges for cloned voice usage on a pay-as-you-go basis, see Amazon Polly Pricing page for details.

Press play: the first few seconds are a real recording of the speaker, and then their cloned voice takes over with words they never recorded.

Voice creation takes minutes, so treat it as a provisioning step, not part of a user request. A small workflow (for example, AWS Step Functions or a job queue) can call CreateVoice, check GetVoice every 30 seconds until the voice is active, and then store the voice ID in your configuration. CreateVoice and DeleteVoice share a limit of 1 request per second, so queue bulk creations and deletions.

Sources indicate that describeVoices lists your cloned voices alongside the voices built into Amazon Polly. Cloned voices have Origin set to customer. Store the voice ID, Region, language, consent reference, and owner for each voice, and give each voice a unique Name. If a CreateVoice call times out or returns a server error, the voice might already exist, and the SDK’s automatic retries can create a second one. Check DescribeVoices for that name before you try again.

CreateVoice returns ThrottlingException when you exceed the 1-request-per-second limit it shares with DeleteVoice, and ServiceQuotaExceededException when the account has reached its cloned-voice quota in that Region. Synthesizing with, or deleting, a voice that is still being created returns ConflictException. If a voice uses your own KMS key, callers without access to the key get an access-denied error. When creation fails, StatusReason gives only a general message, so check the recordings against the preceding recording best practices, then delete the failed voice and create a newly introduced one.

Cloned voices are IAM resources, so you can give the role that serves traffic permission to synthesize with one voice of choice. If the voice uses your own KMS key, that role also needs access to the key. Keep polly:CreateVoice and polly:DeleteVoice for a separate provisioning role. In a recent development, with tags, you can write the same rule once for a whole group of voices using aws:ResourceTag conditions. Review your existing policies too: a statement that allows polly:SynthesizeSpeech on all resources ("Resource": "*") also covers every cloned voice in the account.

Each account has an initial quota of 10 cloned voices per Region. To request an increase, contact your account team or AWS Support. A voice exists only in the account and Region where you created it. To serve another Region or account, create the voice again in that Region from the same recordings, and make sure the speaker’s consent covers every copy.

Before a voice goes live, listen to it reading your own content: product names, numbers, addresses, and the longest sentences you have. The same text can come out slightly differently from one request to the next. For fixed prompts such as phone-line greetings, generate the audio once, approve it with the speaker and your brand team, and serve the stored file. If a name or term comes out wrong, spell it differently in the text or use a Speech Synthesis Markup Language (SSML) tag. Phoneme tags work with cloned voices in English, French, Italian, German, and Spanish. See Supported SSML tags.

Voice cloning in Amazon Polly gives your application a voice people recognize: recorded once, with the speaker’s consent, and ready to use through the Amazon Polly APIs. To get started on the console, make sure Voice cloning is enabled for your account, prepare a recording and the required consent, open the Amazon Polly console, and create your first cloned voice. If you are a upcoming customer and would like to enable voice cloning, reach out to your AWS account team or apply for access to get access.
