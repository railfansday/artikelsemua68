Fooling the Mind Twice: Human Detection of Audiovisual Deepfakes Under Repeated Exposure and Corrective Feedback

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (258 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2611.27080v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Human deepfake detection remains unreliable, and whether reassessment with corrective feedback improves subsequent judgments remains unclear. This study examined differences between initial and repeated deepfake assessments among 110 participants using audiovisual stimuli from the FakeAVCeleb and Deepfake Detection Challenge (DFDC) datasets. Participants evaluated 40 videos from each dataset in phase 1 (PhaseI) and reassessed the same videos in phase 2 (PhaseII) under a procedure combining repeated presentation and corrective feedback. For the FakeAVCeleb dataset, the participants' mean accuracy increased by 4.68 percentage points from 63.30 in PhaseI to 67.98 in PhaseII (t(109)=5.61, p<.001, d_z=0.54). A judgment-level logistic regression with standard errors clustered by participant and video produced a consistent result: beta=0.208, SE=0.040, z=5.23, p<.001, {OR}=1.23. For the DFDC dataset, the participants' mean accuracy increased by 8.64 percentage points from 59.52% in PhaseI to 68.16% in PhaseII (t(109)=7.37, p<.001, d_z=0.70); the logistic model yielded: beta=0.375, SE=0.065, z=5.78, p<.001, {OR}=1.46. Improvements primarily came from fewer false negatives, while false-positive rates remained unchanged. However, judgment reversals occurred in 27.55% of FakeAVCeleb and 32.91% of DFDC assessments, with some initially correct judgments becoming incorrect upon reassessment. Despite the accuracy gains, repeated assessment did not eliminate judgment instability or residual error. Participants also exhibited consistent overconfidence, with mean self-rated confidence exceeding actual accuracy by 11.99 percentage points for FakeAVCeleb and 12.82 percentage points for DFDC across both phases. These findings indicate that repetition with corrective feedback moderately improves deepfake detection but does not fully resolve the limitations of human judgment.
